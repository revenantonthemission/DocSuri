# U2 security and recovery design

Date: 2026-10-13. Unit: `u2-account-personal-controls`. This embedded library extends the shared application through AccountAccess, Library, Personalization and UsageEntitlements. The designs below cover all 39 derived requirements; they do not assert implementation, provider readiness, policy approval or measured conformance.

## SD2-01 — Authentication and current authority

AccountAccess is the sole source of account, session, role, MFA and consent authority. Each domain owner applies that authority to its own target. The browser cannot supply an authoritative owner, role, verified flag or usage quantity. Allowlisted transport schemas reject extra fields, invalid vocabulary and oversized input before expensive work. Private list queries begin with the derived owner constraint; filtering a cross-owner result afterward is prohibited.

Use server-held sessions with separate full-session, primary-authentication/MFA-continuation and reactivation-status scopes. A limited continuation can invoke only its named flow/operation and cannot be upgraded by a browser flag. Each binds flow identity, purpose, owner where established, authority revision, issuance and finite expiry. Full sign-in rotates the session identifier; revocation makes old sessions unusable independent of cleanup completion. Reauthentication, expiry, attempt and affected-session policies are versioned inputs under G1, not inherited defaults.

At each read and effect, resolve current account state and scope. Current administrator role plus completed MFA is required for administrative effects and their receipt reads. No application session confers PlatformControl authority. Unknown authority causes denial/unavailability, never cached permission fallback.

For effects, use the existing local PostgreSQL transaction facility and an AccountAccess-owned authority guard. The owner transaction obtains a guard through the typed C02 boundary and holds its guard revision/lock until its own commit; revocation/consent changes serialize against the same authority record. Lock order is authority guard, owner control row, operation/receipt row, then stable sorted child keys. AccountAccess exposes guard participation, not its writer or unrestricted connection. The exact C02/U1 binding and deadlock behavior must be coordinated before code. Cross-owner or asynchronous effects instead revalidate current authority at their own commit boundary; a caller's earlier guard is not transferable permission. Do not describe separate owner commits as one distributed transaction.

Anonymous search retains its existing boundary. Public account initiation is existence-neutral in payload and externally observable workflow; delivery and expensive hashing run behind bounded admission. Avoid revealing whether an address exists through response reasons, pagination, count fields or a different recovery route. Test timing distributions and abuse responses; do not promise perfect constant-time network behavior.

## SD2-02 — Credential, proof and MFA protection

Use established password/proof libraries; evaluate the carried-forward Argon2id candidate against measured hash memory/concurrency and pinned dependency guidance before selecting parameters. Store algorithm/parameter versions with password hashes, never plaintext or reversible passwords. Rehash only after successful verification within a bounded worker budget. G1 and compatibility/continuity evidence must resolve parameters and legacy upgrade handling before affected operation. No custom cryptographic routine or silent weaker fallback is allowed.

High-entropy random session, reset and delivery tokens are generated by a maintained cryptographic library; store verifier digests when the original need not be recovered. Proof records bind purpose, flow, intended identity/recipient, authority/lifecycle revision, expiry and consumption outcome. Verification consumes the proof and commits its intended owner effect/receipt atomically. A concurrent or superseded proof cannot cause a second effect. Matching retries require the same validated flow plus current allowed authority; knowledge of an account ID is insufficient. Expired or revoked flows never disclose a success receipt solely by guessing a key.

Email replacement retains the old verified identity until verification and uniqueness checks commit. Credential changes apply the selected session-revocation policy in the same authority transaction. Challenge/delivery creation is durable before acknowledgement; external send happens through an owned responsibility with known/unknown disposition. Unknown delivery is not evidence of failure or authorization to flood retries.

MFA enrollment starts inactive. AccountAccess stores an encrypted seed and hashed recovery-code verifiers, each bound to owner, enrollment and key version. A maintained authenticated-encryption primitive protects material that must be recovered for TOTP verification; key custody, backup and rotation are separate from ciphertext and must be concretely bound in Infrastructure Design. A missing/wrong key blocks the operation and creates actionable readiness evidence, with no plaintext fallback.

The single-use material-delivery capability requires recent proof and the correct enrollment. Atomically mark it consumed before returning the protected no-store response. The response can contain seed/QR input and recovery codes only for this narrow purpose; it is excluded from ordinary receipt payloads, logs, tracing, URLs, persistent browser storage and third-party QR services. If delivery is lost, a new enrollment invalidates the old seed/codes; replay cannot display them. Confirm a factor before activation. Valid TOTP verification records its accepted time-step under the configured clock policy to prevent reuse in the same challenge context. Recovery codes are atomically consumed. MFA recovery policy remains G1-blocked; no administrator bypass is introduced.

## SD2-03 — Browser and provider boundaries

For eventual public HTTPS, use host-only Secure, HttpOnly session/continuation cookies with explicit path and expiry; use the `__Host-` naming rules for the full-session cookie where compatible with the selected host binding. SameSite=Lax is the design default for top-level identity returns, subject to actual provider callback verification. A provider requiring a different return method needs an explicit scoped-flow binding and review, not a global SameSite weakening. The isolated loopback profile cannot silently become public configuration.

Protect same-origin mutations with a session/flow-bound CSRF token and origin checks. Missing/invalid protection rejects before effect. Cross-origin CORS is denied unless an explicit origin and necessary credentials policy is configured. Provider callbacks are a narrow exception to browser CSRF-token transport, guarded by their own single-use state, callback and flow bindings; they are not a generic unauthenticated command route. Validate the actual browser path for redirects, cookies and callback method before public activation.

Return locations are registered same-origin view identifiers with bounded non-secret parameters, never arbitrary URLs. Use context-appropriate output encoding, a restrictive CSP with an explicit asset inventory, frame protection, content-type protection and no-store for authentication and private responses. The host owns headers consistently for success, error and redirects. Frontend code does not render untrusted provider/profile text as HTML. Public assets can be cached by content digest; private query/settings/identity/receipt responses cannot enter a shared cache or service-worker cache.

Google and ORCID remain distinct adapters behind AccountAccess. Each flow records configured provider, sign-in/link intent, redirect, state, permitted scopes, nonce/PKCE as applicable, and current link owner. Validate the provider's actual token/identity protocol, issuer/audience where applicable and callback exchange against current official documentation before implementation selection. Unique provider-subject identity prevents cross-owner link collision; email equality never merges accounts. Unlink requires the approved surviving-access policy and link revision. Remote exchange timeout yields a recorded unknown state; retry/reconciliation follows provider evidence, not a new account or blind repeated exchange. ORCID profile data remains attributed and optional; unavailable enrichment preserves manual choices and skip.

## SD2-04 — Mutation durability, revision and recovery

Each owner persists normalized command identity, intent digest, original accepted time, effect reference, original result and policy identity with the domain effect or accepted responsibility in one owner transaction. Unique identity is scoped by owner, operation and command key. A changed intent with an existing key conflicts. Normalize from validated meaningful fields; never include volatile transport IDs or omit behavior-changing fields.

Ordering is current authorization → matching receipt/intent reconciliation → exact expected revision for a new command → effect plus receipt commit. Browser `expectedRevision` is passed unchanged to the trusted context. A missing revision is validation failure; a stale new mutation returns a safe conflict without a foreign/latest revision leak. The browser reads the designated snapshot and allows an explicit decision; it never silently replaces the revision and retries. Authenticated matching replay returns the original immutable acknowledgement, not evolving cleanup status or protected MFA material.

Transport failure before an observed reply is unknown. Preserve the command identity while the current view survives and reconcile the original operation. After a browser restart, use owner-authorized read models (library, settings, lifecycle or reset discovery below) to establish actual state. Never auto-submit the old action on sign-in return. Persisted owner responsibilities, not browser local storage, are the recovery authority.

Required receipts and outstanding obligations cannot be evicted with optional logs. Retention must define safe duplicate suppression after response-history expiry. A retained tombstone can refuse an expired command identity; it must not turn the same key into fresh work. Unknown policy stops affected admission before evidence is lost.

## SD2-05 — Reset responsibility and authenticated rediscovery

This is the concrete design response to upstream functional finding R-01 and NFR3.3/NFR10.2. Personalization owns a `SettingsResetResponsibility`. Reset acceptance atomically writes its stable ID, original action/acceptedAt, new settings revision, immutable input boundary, policy/manifest identity, initial receipt and pending scope tickets. There is one responsibility per accepted reset. `SettingsResetResult` gains a required `resetId`; its existing disposition remains the disposition **at acceptance**. It is not a live status API. Reads never advance settings revision, input boundary or cleanup.

### Proposed C03 types

These field tables are the required canonical delta, not a claim that the existing contract has already changed. Reuse canonical `Id`, `Revision`, `Time`, `PolicyRef`, `PageRequest` and `PageInfo` primitives. All listed fields are required unless explicitly nullable. Reject undeclared fields. Browser inputs omit internal context; the host derives it.

| Type | Fields and semantics |
|---|---|
| `SettingsResetListInput` | `context: CommandContext`, `view: outstanding or history`, `page: PageRequest`. No supplied account ID, mutation key or revision. Outstanding includes pending/blocked work regardless of age; history includes retained completed work. |
| `SettingsResetInspectInput` | `context: CommandContext`, `resetId: Id`. Scope is current full-session owner; ordinary app administration does not grant private reset visibility. |
| `SettingsResetSummary` | `resetId: Id`, `action: delete_behavior or reset_profile`, `acceptedAt: Time`, `acceptedSettingsRevision: Revision`, `inputBoundary: Revision`, `policyRef: PolicyRef`, `manifestRevision: Revision`, `statusRevision: Revision`, `observedAt: Time`, `disposition: pending or blocked or complete`, `reasonKey: Id`. Acceptance fields are immutable. Status revision is a monotonically increasing owner-issued revision for this responsibility, not the shared settings revision. |
| `SettingsResetScopeStatus` | `participantId: Id`, `scopeId: Id`, `manifestRevision: Revision`, `workGeneration: Revision`, `disposition: pending or blocked or complete`, `reasonKey: Id`, `evidenceRef: Id or null`, `updatedAt: Time`. References and reason keys are sanitized owner-visible projections, not paths, payloads or secrets. |
| `SettingsResetStatus` | `reset: SettingsResetSummary`, `scopes: bounded array of SettingsResetScopeStatus`. Required scopes are a finite, validated manifest with an enforced maximum; never truncate them. If the manifest exceeds policy, reject new acceptance rather than silently omit status. |
| `SettingsResetPage` | `items: bounded array of SettingsResetSummary`, `page: PageInfo`, `appliedLimit: positive integer`, `policyRef: PolicyRef`. No cross-owner counts. Empty successful outstanding page means no currently outstanding responsibility in that read, not proof that a particular unknown submission failed. |

### Proposed C03 operations and C13 browser binding

| Operation | Browser route and result | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `settings.listResets` | `GET /api/v2/c03/settings-resets?view=outstanding|history&limit=...&cursor=...` → `SettingsResetPage` | Fresh current full-session authorization; mandatory view, bounded limit and opaque cursor. No mutation key required. Read only. |
| `settings.inspectReset` | `GET /api/v2/c03/settings-resets/{resetId}` → `SettingsResetStatus` | Current owner match before projection or existence details. Foreign and absent IDs share the non-revealing not-found response. Read only. |
| `settings.reset` | Existing `POST /api/v2/c03/settings-reset` → amended `SettingsResetResult` | Preserve explicit confirmation, policy, top-level expectedRevision and idempotency; return resetId. Duplicate authorized command returns the same original receipt and ID, never another boundary. |

List order is acceptedAt descending then resetId descending, with unique tie-breaking and a server-generated high-water tuple in the opaque cursor. Cursor binds owner, authority generation, view, last tuple, high-water tuple, policy and finite expiry; authenticate it or store only an opaque random lookup token. Subsequent pages keep the acceptance high-water mark, reapply current authority and current disposition filters, and may reflect progress changes. This is a traversal bound, not a frozen status snapshot. New acceptances require a fresh first page. Completed rows can disappear from outstanding; retained history supplies their status. A remembered selection uses inspect directly. On restart, show outstanding first and allow history browsing; neither requires prior browser state. Do not infer that work vanished from one filtered page because it completed.

Responses use success only after a completed authoritative read. Invalid page/cursor shape is validation error; expired/stale cursor has an explicit refresh-required error; expired authentication requires fresh sign-in; store or authority unavailability is unavailable, never an empty page. Use the canonical safe error envelope/status mapping during C13 coordination. Reject tampered/foreign cursors without exposing their owner or counts. Polling is bounded with backoff and pauses while hidden/offline; budget exhaustion stops automatic refresh with a manual read-only refresh control. A read cannot enqueue a worker or extend a lease.

Status is derived from every required policy/manifest-bound scope acknowledgement. `blocked` means unresolved evidence or exhausted automatic retry; it preserves responsibility. `complete` requires all scopes complete, including required backup/restore restrictions. Replacement claims advance workGeneration; old acknowledgements cannot satisfy current work. Evidence that invalidates prior completion reopens the same responsibility with a newer statusRevision and blocked disposition; it never replays reset. A newer reset does not implicitly complete an older one: reusable evidence must explicitly prove each boundary/scope obligation.

UI keys status by owner authority generation plus resetId. Within one ID, discard responses older than the highest applied statusRevision; never compare opaque settings revisions numerically. Across IDs, keep separate cards and acceptance ordering. A response for an older ID cannot complete the selected newer reset. Account switch clears all cards and in-flight responses. Use Korean labels for accepted/pending/blocked/complete; only an authoritative complete projection displays completed cleanup.

Outstanding work remains discoverable until its obligations and permitted retention are resolved. G3 must define history retention, minimized tombstones and backup enforcement; history expiry cannot remove suppression/reconciliation still needed by delayed events or restores. If mandatory evidence storage approaches its bound, stop new acceptance and preserve recovery capacity. No arbitrary TTL is chosen here.

### Coordination and closure before implementation

Before reset persistence/status code: amend C03 definitions/operations and C13 schemas/routes/error matrix; regenerate affected bindings; inventory actual consumers; coordinate required `resetId` as a breaking result-shape change where consumers reject extra fields. Do not claim additive wire compatibility merely because a field was added. Preserve legacy behavior through an explicit adapter only if consumer evidence requires it. Use the approved coordinated-version policy and contract validator.

Then verify lost acknowledgement, browser-state loss, fresh owner return, two resets, pending→blocked→complete, reordered responses, foreign reads/cursors, expired cursor, store outage and zero writes/new boundaries from list/inspect. Until canonical and executable evidence exist, upstream R-01 remains open for implementation closure; this design establishes the missing mechanism.

## SD2-06 — Consent, clear/reset, lifecycle and restore

AccountAccess consent is authoritative at admission and effect. Library history clear records the original trusted Discovery admission checkpoint. Personalization reset records its input boundary separately from settings-control revision. Delayed activity at or below an applicable clear boundary, from a prohibited reset generation, or with currently revoked purpose is suppressed; client wall-clock timestamps cannot establish eligibility. Duplicate clear/reset returns its original boundary and cannot remove subsequently eligible work. Regrant permits only newly eligible input; it cannot resurrect erased events. Explicit library references, history, defaults and committed research work remain distinct ownership/scope decisions.

Lifecycle requests capture an immutable acceptance time, selected policy and complete mounted manifest. Authority revocation commits before cleanup can be reported, and pending cleanup cannot authorize new effects. Required participants cover live records, projections/caches, accepted work, external copies and backup/restore enforcement where applicable. Validate unique owner/scope assignments and supported contract versions before acceptance. Missing/incompatible participants block the dependent command; they do not reduce the manifest to whatever happens to be running.

Each owner uses durable tickets and generation-fenced claims. Only the currently claimed generation can commit cleanup evidence; a timeout replaces a claim, not its responsibility or original acceptance. A coordinator aggregates explicit acknowledgements and rejects stale policy/manifest/generation evidence. Bounded retry with backoff eventually exposes blocked/manual-recovery state; operators resume through the owning contract and cannot fabricate completion. New participant generations need explicit reconciliation against the original obligation; unrelated new scope is not silently retroactive policy.

Withdrawal, erasure and reactivation remain distinct. No withdrawn full session can poll private state; use only the contract's purpose-bound status continuation or current separately authorized audit access. Reactivation restores only demonstrably retained permissible records, then requires fresh normal login and MFA. Restore occurs in isolation against current authority, consent, tombstones, input boundaries and the exact restore set. If those current records are unavailable or older than the required restriction evidence, quarantine/deny serving until reconciliation; an old backup is not authority to roll them back. No legacy 30-day interval or unapproved retention is selected.

## SD2-07 — Usage and external-effect reconciliation

UsageEntitlements owns the ledger, finite trusted quantity, server period and assignment revision. Serialize allowance/reservation decisions at the owning balance boundary; atomically record reservation plus original operation identity so concurrent last-allowance requests cannot overspend. Reads and absent-assignment inspection never allocate; an absent assignment has the canonical non-null initial revision. Grants/revokes require current administrator/MFA authority and target assignment revision, with reason and durable receipt. Tier display remains mock/administrative with no payment effect.

Separate operation acceptance and accounting commits use a durable protocol: reserve under the stable operation identity, persist the accepting owner's outcome/backreference, then reconcile settlement. Crashes between these steps leave visible responsibility. Query the authoritative accepting owner before release/refund; timeout, missing telemetry or lease expiry alone is not proof of no acceptance. Settlement is idempotent by original operation/reservation; conflicting settlement evidence blocks for reconciliation. Late completion stays bound to original period/policy. G6 must settle policy changes, refunds and accepted-work treatment before real allocation; no assumed zero balance, unlimited allowance or guessed refund.

## SD2-08 — Privacy, safe evidence and repair

Classify identity/profile, credential/proof/session material, library/query/history, preferences, behavior, receipts, ledger, diagnostics and backups by owning purpose/access/location/retention before real-data use. G3 must reconcile minimization with outstanding responsibility and restore restrictions. This is a design control, not a claim of compliance with a particular law or provider license.

Allowlisted diagnostic events contain correlation ID, operation kind, owner pseudonymous reference where authorized, policy/revision, responsibility/participant generation, acceptance/observation time, disposition, bounded reason key and duration. Exclude passwords, seed/codes/tokens, email/query/manuscript text, raw provider bodies, full URLs and unfiltered exceptions. Diagnostic references are not bearer authority. Operators receive least-privileged views; user views contain only their safe status. Test redaction on errors and retries, not just success.

Optional logs/metrics have finite queues, byte limits and retention with drop/rotation counters. Authority, receipts, tombstones and unresolved-work records are business state and cannot be sacrificed to log rotation. Readiness is an owner-supplied capability projection: unavailable policy/key/adapter/authority/store or participant yields a safe reason and disabled affected operation. Liveness, mounted routes and synthetic sink success do not imply public readiness. Local synthetic readiness and real-provider readiness are separate fields.

A reproducible defect packet identifies commit/build, lockfile, policy versions, isolated fixture seed, operation/reference IDs, fault schedule, sanitized state transitions and exact command. One maintainer must be able to reproduce, repair, roll back or reconcile and recheck without copying private payloads into a bug report. Measure diagnosis/repair time; agree numeric targets after baseline. Dependency updates require license/security/compatibility/resource evidence, migrations with restore reconciliation, and explicit safe-stop conditions.

## SD2-09 — Accessible workspace and safe browser return

Reuse the approved Korean responsive workspace, inherited blue/neutral tokens, Apple HIG web hierarchy and Light/Dark/System. Preserve keyboard order/focus, visible labels and field-linked errors; announce status without focus theft. Pending, blocked, unknown and failed use text as well as color. Destructive confirmation states the exact scope, preserved data and whether acceptance or completion is being requested. Account and credential forms make no durable research-draft promise.

Each private request carries a client authority-generation tag. Expiry/logout/account switch increments that generation, clears private component/query caches and aborts requests; all handlers also reject late old-generation results. Coordinate tabs with a non-secret invalidation signal and revalidate on focus/pageshow before revealing private cached state. Browser restart starts without private account state and obtains current session authority before reads. No secrets/private forms in local/session storage, history state or service-worker caches. Safe return restores the view only, never a mutation.

Preserve non-secret in-flight edits for the current authorized view on validation/conflict while showing their unsaved state. Clear them when authority changes. Manual onboarding/skip and explicit defaults remain usable during unavailable enrichment; unsupported modes stay visibly unsupported rather than silently rewritten. Verify desktop/tablet/phone layouts, keyboard/screen-reader flows, zoom/reflow, contrast and accessible authentication; design review does not establish WCAG conformance.

## Verification and evidence prerequisites

Implement the nine scenarios V1–V9 in [technology decisions](../nfr-requirements/tech-stack-decisions.md#measurement-and-verification), using mounted routes and real owner stores. SD2-01–09 and LC2-01–08 supply their mechanisms; the traceability file supplies exact NFR coverage. Assertions must inspect effects, boundaries, dispositions and persisted evidence in addition to HTTP status. Faults include crash before/after commit, lost response, revocation race, replaced worker, provider uncertainty, full disk and missing participant. Do not treat this document's checks as runtime success.

G1 credentials/MFA policy; G2 provider/email eligibility and real delivery; G3 authoritative data/retention/backups; G4 vocabularies/access matrix; G5 installed manifests; G6 quota/settlement; G7 finite resource fit and measured targets; G8 canonical coordination remain the upstream evidence gates. Designs may proceed with explicit synthetic policies. Real affected activation requires closure evidence and the human decisions specified by those gates. No paid fallback, public hosting selection or silent feature removal is authorized here.
