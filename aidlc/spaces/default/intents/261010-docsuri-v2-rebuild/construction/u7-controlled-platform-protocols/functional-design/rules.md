# U7 business rules

## Scope and source of truth

These rules govern the retained controlled operator executable. They realize
the acceptance statements of the platform-evidence and controlled-operation
requirements ([requirements](../../../inception/requirements-analysis/requirements.md),
FR20.1 and FR20.2) and the C10 contract
([contract summary](../../../inception/contract-design/contract-summary.md)).
The fenced `yaml` block is the source of truth for the rule set; the table after
it is a derived summary. Rules fix behaviour, not storage or transport
technology.

Answers carried in from [Functional Design questions](functional-design-questions.md):
Q1 complete inventory with per-protocol dispositions; Q2 local OS identity with
a target-scoped grant; Q3 a separate pre-provisioned grant for
destructive/release; Q4 binding + generation + finite maximum age with a
read-only U8 interface; Q5 explicit disposition for cloud-dependent protocols;
Q6 canonical v2 envelope, legacy compatibility only where a real consumer
exists; Q7 the `authorized → executing → completed | failed | unknown` ladder
with reconciliation.

## Interpretation and implementation handoff

- Storage, transport implementation and native adapter mechanics are out of scope
  for this design; the rules constrain behaviour and refuse-by-default posture.
- The concrete inventory (BR5.1/BR5.2) and the per-protocol `maxAge` (BR1.2) are
  populated during implementation from the retained-reference reconciliation;
  this design fixes the disposition rule, not the final list.
- No rule authorizes running a real destructive or release operation; BR3.7
  confines validation to synthetic fixtures under explicit operator authority.
